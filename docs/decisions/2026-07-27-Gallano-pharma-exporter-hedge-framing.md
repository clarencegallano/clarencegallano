## Executive Summary (≤150 words)
The U.S. Pharmaceutical Exporter holds a EUR 8,000,000 receivable due in 12 months, exposed to EUR/USD fluctuation between contract inception and settlement. At current spot (1.1363), the receivable is worth USD 9,090,400; at the indicative 12-month forward (1.0890), it converts to a certain USD 8,712,000 — a difference of roughly USD 378,000. This gap reflects the USD–EUR one-year rate differential (4.14% vs. 2.727%, ~141 bps), not a market forecast. Our objective is to hedge this exposure using a forward, money market hedge, or options — each offering a different balance of certainty, cost, and upside participation. Selecting the right instrument protects shareholder value amid an unpredictable economic and political landscape. This is Stage 1 of five; Stage 2 will price all three alternatives against firm quotes.

---

## Background & Objectives
The exporter is subject to transaction exposure: its EUR-denominated receivable will be converted to USD at an unknown future rate. Recent ECB tightening and sustained USD strength have widened the one-year rate differential to ~141 bps, pulling the forward rate (1.0890) 4.2% below spot (1.1363) and exposing an unhedged position to a potential USD 378,000 shortfall. The primary objective is to hedge the transaction exposure using a forward, money market hedge, or options contract. The secondary goals are to maximize shareholder wealth and add firm value amid volatile FX and geopolitical conditions.

## Methods
We are evaluating three hedge families side by side:

Three hedge families are under evaluation: (1) Forward/Futures — locks in 1.0890 (USD 8,712,000), providing certainty but eliminating upside if the euro strengthens; (2) Money Market Hedge — borrow EUR, convert at spot, invest at the USD 4.14% rate, repaying the loan at maturity; offers similar certainty and is useful when forwards are unavailable or mispriced, but adds complexity and depends on borrowing capacity; (3) Options (EUR Put) — caps downside while preserving upside, for an upfront premium. Stage 2 will model all-in net proceeds for each under multiple scenarios.
